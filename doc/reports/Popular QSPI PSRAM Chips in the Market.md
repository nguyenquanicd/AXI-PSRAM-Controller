# QSPI PSRAM: Opcode Convergence, Protocol Fragmentation

This research confirms that the real QSPI/SPI PSRAM market converges around a "de facto" opcode set — Read ID `0x9F`, Reset Enable/Reset `0x66`/`0x99`, Fast Read Quad `0xEB`, Quad Write `0x38`, Enter/Exit QPI `0x35`/`0xF5`, Wrap Boundary Toggle `0xC0` — repeated almost identically across four independent sources: AP Memory APS6404L/APS1604M-SQ, Espressif-branded PSRAM (ESP-PSRAM64/64H), Lyontek LY68L6400, and ISSI's SerialRAM (WVS) family. But the initially assumed manufacturer list needed significant correction once checked against primary datasheets: Winbond currently sells **no** opcode-style QSPI/QPI PSRAM at all — its entire PSRAM catalog is HyperBus/HyperRAM, a completely different DDR protocol with no single-byte command; Puya, Dosilicon, Etron, and GigaDevice do not manufacture PSRAM; Alliance Memory and Vilsion Technology only sell 16-bit parallel PSRAM (not QSPI, and already EOL); and Zentel has been acquired by AP Memory and is no longer an independent supplier. More notably, even within ISSI itself, the **WVQ** "QuadRAM" family uses a completely different nibble-based command set — no `0x9F`, no Enter/Exit-QPI — compared to the **WVS** family from the same manufacturer; and within AP Memory, two Octal variants sharing the same base part number APS6408L — OctaRAM and Xccela — swap the meaning of the very same opcode values. In the real embedded/IoT ecosystem, AP Memory's APS6404L (and pin/command-compatible variants) is the most common silicon, officially used or recommended as a substitute by ESP32, RP2350, and STM32 Discovery boards. Cross-checked against the internal "QSPI/SPI PSRAM Controller" RTL IP, the controller covers the most common SDR Quad/SPI protocol layer well — configurable 1/2-byte opcodes, an independent command for SPI↔QSPI mode switching, and runtime read/write command swapping — but has no support at all for 8-bit Octal or DDR, meaning it cannot talk to AP Memory OctaRAM/Xccela, ISSI WVO, any HyperBus/HyperRAM device, or even ISSI's WVQ family despite the latter using only 4 IO wires.

## AP Memory is the real pivot of the market, not one of six evenly-spread vendors

After verifying each candidate against primary sources, the list of QSPI/SPI PSRAM manufacturers that actually exist today is much narrower than initially assumed. **AP Memory** has the broadest and most fully confirmed product range: the classic QSPI lines APS6404L (64Mb) and APS1604M-SQ (16Mb) run SPI/QPI SDR up to 144MHz, plus two protocol-incompatible 64Mb Octal lines — OctaRAM (APS6408L-3OCx, proprietary, 3.3V) and Xccela (APS6408L-OBMx, JEDEC-compatible, 1.8V) — and an OPI/HPI family extending up to 512Mb per the official product page ([AP Memory APS6404L datasheet](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112120r9601773), [APS1604M-SQ datasheet](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112120r6586620), [APS6408L-3OCx datasheet](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112221xb586549), [APS6408L-OBMx datasheet](https://datasheet.octopart.com/APS6408L-OBM-BA-AP-Memory-datasheet-182363276.pdf), [AP Memory OPI/HPI product page](https://www.apmemory.com/en/product/iotram/OPIHPI)). **ISSI** sells PSRAM under three completely separate protocol families under the same "IS66/67" prefix: **WVQ** (QuadRAM, Quad-DDR-only, 32/64Mb), **WVS** (SerialRAM, classic SPI+QPI, 8Mb — with sibling 16/32Mb densities inferred from the ID register's density field but not independently datasheet-confirmed), and **WVO/WVH** (Octal-IO and HyperRAM) ([ISSI WVQ8M4 datasheet](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVQ8M4FALL-BLL.pdf), [ISSI WVS1M8 datasheet](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVS1M8ALL-BLL.pdf), [ISSI product page](https://www.issi.com/US/product-serial-sram-and-serial-ram.shtml)). **Lyontek** (Taiwan) is a real, currently-producing manufacturer with LY68L6400 (64Mb, 3.3V, 100MHz) and LY68S6400 (64Mb, 1.8V, 143MHz) — although a 2024–2025 EEVblog thread notes LY68L6400 has become hard to source through mainstream distribution, which is a supply-chain issue rather than evidence of discontinuation ([Lyontek product page](https://www.lyontek.com.tw/en/serialsram.html), [EEVblog thread](https://www.eevblog.com/forum/projects/what-happened-to-lyontek-ly68l6400-psram/)).

By contrast, **Winbond has no opcode-style QSPI/QPI PSRAM whatsoever**: a review of all 25 documents in the manufacturer's "PSRAM" catalog turns up only HyperRAM/HyperBus parts (W955D8MBYA, W956D8MBYA, W955N8MBYA, W955K8MBYA...) plus one legacy asynchronous CRAM part (W956D6KBKX) — none use byte opcodes like 0x9F/0xEB/0x35 ([Winbond documentation portal](https://www.winbond.com/hq/support/documentation/?__locale=en&line=%2Fproduct%2Fcustomized-memory-solution%2Findex.html&family=%2Fproduct%2Fcustomized-memory-solution%2Fpsram%2Findex.html&category=%2F.categories%2Fresources%2Fdatasheet%2F)). Other candidates from the initial assumed list were all ruled out after checking their official product pages directly: **Puya** (Flash/EEPROM/MCU only, no PSRAM), **Dosilicon** (NAND/NOR/DDR3 only, no PSRAM), **Etron** (historically tied to the PSRAM/CellularRAM concept, but no PSRAM in its current lineup), **GigaDevice** (no standalone PSRAM product), and **"ZD Semiconductor"** / **"Vitesa Technology"** (no such companies could be found to exist) — all excluded from the final list rather than guessed at ([Puya homepage](https://www.puyasemi.com/en/), [Dosilicon homepage](https://www.dosilicon.com/), [Etron homepage](https://etron.com/)). **Alliance Memory** and **Vilsion Technology (VTI)** do genuinely sell PSRAM, but both are 16-bit parallel SRAM-style interfaces (not serial SPI/QSPI), and Alliance Memory's line is now listed under "EOL" on its own product page ([Alliance Memory product overviews](https://www.alliancememory.com/product-overviews/), [Vilsion pSRAM product list](https://www.vilsion.com/list-14-1.html)). **Zentel Electronics**, a real Taiwanese PSRAM/DRAM manufacturer, was acquired by AP Memory, and its PSRAM part numbers (APS6404L-SQ-SN) are now part of AP Memory's own APM IoTRAM™ line — so it is not counted as a separate independent supplier ([Zentel ball-assignment doc](https://zentel-europe.com/pdf/Ball%20assignment%20of%20APM%20PSRAM%20and%20LPDRAM%20products%20Q2.pdf)). **XTX Technology** (Shenzhen) is confirmed via its own news page to sell a PSRAM line (the XT70 series, packaged together with NOR flash in an MCP), but its datasheet PDF could not be retrieved through any method attempted — so XT70's opcodes, dummy cycles, wrap-burst behavior, and voltage remain **unverified** and are recorded as a research gap rather than guessed at ([XTX news page](https://www.xtxtech.com/en/newsandevents/info.aspx?itemid=137)).

## A "de facto" opcode set repeated nearly identically across four independent sources

The command table below — Read `0x03`, Fast Read `0x0B`, Fast Read Quad `0xEB`, Write `0x02`, Quad Write `0x38`, Enter QPI `0x35`, Exit QPI `0xF5`, Reset Enable `0x66`, Reset `0x99`, Wrap Boundary Toggle `0xC0`, Read ID `0x9F` — appears nearly byte-for-byte identical across four independent datasheets read directly for this research: AP Memory APS6404L-SQH ([datasheet §9.5](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112120r9601773)), Espressif-branded ESP-PSRAM64/64H ([datasheet §4–8](https://cdn-shop.adafruit.com/product-files/4677/4677_esp-psram64_esp-psram64h_datasheet_en.pdf)), Lyontek LY68L6400 ([datasheet §10.5](https://community.nxp.com/pwmxy87654/attachments/pwmxy87654/kinetis/41021/1/LY68L6400-0.4.pdf)), and ISSI IS66/67WVS1M8 ([datasheet Table 4.1](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVS1M8ALL-BLL.pdf)). Notably, the Known-Good-Die table in ESP-PSRAM64/64H (`0b0101_0101`=Fail, `0b0101_1101`=Pass) matches AP Memory's own equivalent table almost verbatim — a vendor-specific implementation detail unlikely to have been independently invented twice — and Espressif's ESP-IDF source names its PSRAM driver files `esp_quad_psram_defs_ap.h`/`esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c` (the "_ap" suffix) and checks a vendor ID of `0xD`, exactly matching the "APM" code published in AP Memory's own datasheet — strong circumstantial evidence (though not a direct written statement) that Espressif-branded PSRAM is in fact AP Memory silicon ([esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c, espressif/esp-idf](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_psram/device/esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c)).

Dummy-cycle counts per command are also surprisingly consistent across the four sources: Read `0x03` = 0 dummy; Fast Read `0x0B` = 8 dummy cycles in SPI mode but only 4 once already in QPI mode (exactly the "same opcode, different dummy count depending on mode" behavior a host controller must handle); Fast Read Quad `0xEB` = 6 dummy cycles in both modes; write commands `0x02`/`0x38` = 0 dummy cycles at every frequency ([AP Memory APS6404L §9.5](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112120r9601773); [Lyontek §10.5](https://community.nxp.com/pwmxy87654/attachments/pwmxy87654/kinetis/41021/1/LY68L6400-0.4.pdf); [ISSI WVS1M8 Table 4.1](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVS1M8ALL-BLL.pdf)). For these parts, dummy cycles are **fixed per opcode**, with no Mode Register to configure them — in sharp contrast to the Octal DDR generation (see below). Wrap burst on this group is similarly simple: a single toggle command `0xC0` switches between 1024-byte wrap (default) and 32-byte wrap, with no independent register to read the state back — the host controller must keep its own "shadow" state since the device offers no way to read the current value ([AP Memory APS6404L §9.2](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112120r9601773); [ESP-PSRAM64/64H §4](https://cdn-shop.adafruit.com/product-files/4677/4677_esp-psram64_esp-psram64h_datasheet_en.pdf)). APS1604M-SQ and ISSI WVQ go one step further, putting wrap-length into a writable Mode/Configuration Register (MR0[6:5] or CR[1:0]) instead of a single binary toggle flag — allowing 16/32/64/512B (APS1604M) or 16/32/64/128B (ISSI WVQ) to be selected ([APS1604M-SQ datasheet Table 3/4](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112120r6586620); [ISSI WVQ8M4 Table 6.1](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVQ8M4FALL-BLL.pdf)).

## Even within a single manufacturer, conflicting protocol families exist

The single most important finding for a controller design is this: **the biggest protocol divergence is not between manufacturers, but inside ISSI and inside AP Memory themselves.** The **ISSI WVQ** family (QuadRAM, IS66/67WVQ8M4/16M4) does not use the "de facto" opcode set above at all — it uses a completely different **nibble** (4-bit) command set: Memory READ continuous-burst = `Ah`, READ wrapped-burst = `8h`, WRITE continuous = `2h`, WRITE wrapped = `0h`, ID/Configuration register read = `Ch`/`Eh`, Configuration Register write = `4h`/`6h` ([ISSI WVQ8M4/16M4 datasheet Table 4.2](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVQ8M4FALL-BLL.pdf)). WVQ has **no** standalone Read ID command `0x9F`, **no** Reset Enable/Reset pair `0x66`/`0x99`, and most importantly — **no concept of Enter/Exit QPI** at all, because the device always runs in Quad DDR mode from power-up and never leaves it. A controller designed around the "de facto" opcode set will be completely unable to talk to WVQ, even though this part number belongs to the same ISSI manufacturer and the same "IS66/67" documentation family as WVS ([ISSI research notes, Inferences section](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVQ8M4FALL-BLL.pdf)).

Within AP Memory itself, two Octal variants sharing the same base part number **APS6408L** — OctaRAM (suffix `-3OCx`, proprietary protocol, 3.3V) and Xccela (suffix `-OBMx`, JEDEC/Xccela-compatible, 1.8V) — **swap the meaning of the exact same opcode values**: on OctaRAM, Sync Read = `0x80` and Sync Write = `0x00`; on Xccela, Sync Read = `0x00` and Sync Write = `0x80` — numerically identical, but with completely reversed meaning ([APS6408L-3OCx datasheet §7.4](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112221xb586549); [APS6408L-OBMx datasheet §8.4](https://datasheet.octopart.com/APS6408L-OBM-BA-AP-Memory-datasheet-182363276.pdf)). This is a concrete, citable example of "same manufacturer, same part-number prefix, completely different opcodes" — exactly the kind of trap a general-purpose controller must guard against by never assuming a protocol family from a part-number pattern alone without checking the actual datasheet. ESP-IDF's source code (`esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c`) confirms the ESP32-S3 Octal PSRAM driver specifically targets **Xccela** (matching opcodes `0x00/0x80/0x20/0xA0/0x40/0xC0`, doubled per DDR edge into 16-bit values `0x0000/0x8080/0x2020/0xA0A0/0x4040/0xC0C0`), not OctaRAM ([esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_psram/device/esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c)).

A third, and probably the most decisive, protocol-family conflict is **HyperBus/HyperRAM** (Winbond W955D8MBYA, and Infineon/Cypress S27KL0642/S27KS0642 used here for comparison): instead of a byte opcode, every transaction begins with three 16-bit Command-Address words (CA0–CA2, 48 bits total) with fixed bit fields — CA[47]=R/W#, CA[46]=address space (memory or register), CA[45]=burst type — meaning **there is no equivalent of a `0x9F`/`0xEB`/`0x38` byte command at all** ([Infineon S27KL0642/S27KS0642 datasheet §4.1, Table 3](https://www.infineon.com/dgdl/Infineon-S27KL0642_S27KS0642_3.0_V_1.8_V_64_Mb_(8_MB)_HyperRAM_Self-Refresh_DRAM-DataSheet-v09_00-EN.pdf?fileId=8ac78c8c7d0d8da4017d0ee8a1c47164); [Winbond W955D8MBYA datasheet §7](https://www.winbond.com/resource-files/W955D8MBYA_32Mb_HyperBus_pSRAM_TFBGA24_datasheet_A01-002_20220916.pdf)). The physical bus differs too: 8 data lines DQ[7:0], a single/differential clock CK(#), and an **RWDS** (Read-Write Data Strobe) pin that has no equivalent on a classic QSPI bus — HyperBus transfers two bytes per clock cycle (genuine DDR), whereas classic QSPI PSRAM is SDR. Reset also differs mechanically: HyperRAM/HyperBus only has a hardware RESET# pin, with no `0x66`/`0x99` software command pair as in the classic opcode family. A controller that only supports 1-bit SPI/4-bit QSPI **cannot communicate at any level** with a HyperBus device, even though both are commonly marketed as "PSRAM."

## Dummy cycles, wrap burst, and tCEM/tCSM: the same mechanism, different numbers per chip

A "hidden/self-managed refresh" mechanism is an absolute commonality across every PSRAM surveyed: no device requires the host to issue a DRAM-style refresh command, but every device imposes a maximum continuous CS#-low time (commonly called tCEM or tCSM) so its internal refresh can run — violating this limit is explicitly warned to "block internal refresh and cause memory errors" ([AP Memory APS6404L datasheet §2](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112120r9601773)). However, **the specific numbers are not standardized across manufacturers**: AP Memory (both its QSPI line and both Octal lines) uses 8µs (standard temperature) / 3µs (extended, ≥105°C); ISSI (both WVQ and WVS) uses 4µs (~85°C) / 1µs (~105°C); Lyontek LY68L6400 uses a noticeably tighter ceiling of just 2µs; Winbond W955D8MBYA and Infineon/Cypress HyperRAM both use 4µs (85°C) / 1µs (105°C) ([ISSI WVQ8M4 §7.6.1/7.6.2](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVQ8M4FALL-BLL.pdf); [Lyontek datasheet §10.6](https://community.nxp.com/pwmxy87654/attachments/pwmxy87654/kinetis/41021/1/LY68L6400-0.4.pdf); [Infineon HyperRAM datasheet §6.3.3.4](https://www.infineon.com/dgdl/Infineon-S27KL0642_S27KS0642_3.0_V_1.8_V_64_Mb_(8_MB)_HyperRAM_Self-Refresh_DRAM-DataSheet-v09_00-EN.pdf?fileId=8ac78c8c7d0d8da4017d0ee8a1c47164)). A general PSRAM guide from Realtek Ameba even cites a different temperature-derating ratio — a 4× drop (4µs→1µs) versus AP Memory's 2× drop (8µs→4µs) — confirming this is **not a unified industry constant**, and a host controller should never hard-code a single "generally safe" value but must read it from each target chip's own datasheet ([Realtek Ameba PSRAM peripheral docs](https://aiot.realmcu.com/en/latest/rtos/peripherals/psram/index.html)).

The "refresh in progress, extra latency needed" signaling mechanism is consistent in principle but different in implementation: AP Memory's and ISSI's Octal families use a DQSM pin to signal doubled latency (LC×2) when a refresh collides with an access; HyperBus uses the RWDS pin for the exact same purpose ([APS6408L-3OCx §7.5](https://www.apmemory.com/tw/downloadFiles/0324112221xb586549); [ISSI WVQ8M4 §7.6.1](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVQ8M4FALL-BLL.pdf); [Infineon HyperRAM §6.3.2.2](https://www.infineon.com/dgdl/Infineon-S27KL0642_S27KS0642_3.0_V_1.8_V_64_Mb_(8_MB)_HyperRAM_Self-Refresh_DRAM-DataSheet-v09_00-EN.pdf?fileId=8ac78c8c7d0d8da4017d0ee8a1c47164)). Sophistication in frequency-dependent dummy-cycle configuration increases by product generation: AP Memory's classic QSPI line has no Mode Register at all (dummy fixed per opcode), but ISSI WVQ, Winbond HyperBus, and both AP Memory Octal families all expose a "Latency Code" field in a Mode/Configuration Register tabulated directly against target SCLK frequency — for example ISSI WVQ's table: CR[7:4]=0000→3 clocks/83MHz, 0001→4/100MHz, 0010→5/133MHz, 0011→6/166MHz, 0100 (default)→7/200MHz, with latency **doubling on a refresh collision** ([ISSI WVQ8M4/16M4 Table 6.5](https://www.issi.com/WW/pdf/66-67WVQ8M4FALL-BLL.pdf)); AP Memory Xccela's equivalent table uses MR0[4:2] for Read Latency (3→66MHz...7→200MHz) and a completely separate MR4[7:5] Write Latency field ([APS6408L-OBMx Tables 5/16](https://datasheet.octopart.com/APS6408L-OBM-BA-AP-Memory-datasheet-182363276.pdf)); and Winbond HyperBus uses CR0[7:4] with 4 discrete points (0000b=5 clocks/≤133MHz default, 0001b=6/≤166MHz, 1110b=3/≤83MHz, 1111b=4/≤104MHz) ([Winbond datasheet Table 8](https://www.winbond.com/resource-files/W955D8MBYA_32Mb_HyperBus_pSRAM_TFBGA24_datasheet_A01-002_20220916.pdf)). ESP-IDF implements exactly this mechanism in software: its Octal PSRAM driver writes the MR0 (read latency) and MR4 (write latency) fields at init time, with dummy-length macros that step up with frequency tier (10/14/18 for default/200MHz/250MHz respectively) ([esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_psram/device/esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c)).

Looking at the real embedded/IoT supply chain, **AP Memory's APS6404L (and pin/command-compatible variants) is the single most common PSRAM silicon** for QSPI designs. On Espressif platforms, the ESP32 community confirms AP Memory APS6404 and IPUS IPS6404 as pin/command-compatible substitutes for Espressif-branded PSRAM (though this particular discussion thread could not be fetched directly and is only confirmed via a search-index summary) ([ESP32 forum thread](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?t=17636)); on ESP32-S3, PSRAM is integrated in-package and Espressif does not disclose the die manufacturer, but the Octal PSRAM driver source itself checks for vendor ID `0xD` = AP Memory ([esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/master/components/esp_psram/device/esp_psram_impl_ap_oct.c)). On **RP2350**, community/manufacturer boards (Adafruit Feather RP2350, Pimoroni Pico Plus 2) use AP Memory APS6404L(-3SQR) through RP2350's QMI (QSPI Memory Interface) block ([RP2350 driver source](https://github.com/Altaflux/rp2350-psram-test/blob/main/src/psram.rs)); RP2350's QMI makes read/write opcode, prefix length, and dummy length all runtime-programmable registers — the closest architecture to an ideal "genuinely configurable general-purpose controller" found in this research. On **STM32**, the official STM32L4P5G-DK Discovery board uses AP Memory APS6408L-3OBx (Octal) ([STM32L4P5G-DK BSP source](https://github.com/STMicroelectronics/stm32l4p5g-dk-bsp/blob/main/stm32l4p5g_discovery_psram.c)), and an AP Memory engineer directly confirmed on an ST community forum that APS6404L-3SQR-SN is the "equivalent part" for ESP-PSRAM64H in non-ESP32 designs ([ST Community thread](https://community.st.com/s/question/0D53W00000NVsy1SAD/esppsram64h-and-stm32h743-qspi-mode)). By contrast, **Nordic nRF5340/nRF91** has no external PSRAM support at all — their QSPI block is hardwired specifically for NOR flash, with no path for PSRAM ([Nordic DevZone Q&A](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/f/nordic-q-a/104403/nrf5340-qspi-and-external-psram)).

Publicly documented compatibility incidents are mostly caused by software policy rather than genuine electrical/opcode conflicts: ESP-IDF actively rejects PSRAM that doesn't carry an Espressif vendor ID even when it may be electrically/command compatible, producing a "PSRAM ID read error" even when the hardware is entirely valid ([esp-idf issue #8863](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/8863)); and a "nibble-shift corruption" incident once suspected to be a QMI configuration bug on RP2350 + APS6404L was ultimately traced by the community to PCB layout (trace length, impedance, grounding) rather than a controller bug, once the correct dummy-cycle values ("6 for read, 0 for write") were confirmed ([RP2350 nibble-shift thread](https://forums.raspberrypi.com/viewtopic.php?t=392957)).

## Cross-checking the internal "QSPI/SPI PSRAM Controller" RTL IP against the market

The internal IP (spec v2.0, AXI4↔QSPI/SPI bridge, Command–Address–Dummy–Data phase FSM) covers exactly what's needed for the most common protocol layer found in this research — the classic SDR Quad/SPI group using the "de facto" opcode set (AP Memory APS6404L/APS1604M, Espressif-branded PSRAM, Lyontek, ISSI WVS). Three direct matches stand out: first, the `ctrl.cmd_2bytes` register lets software select an 8-bit or 16-bit command, exactly matching the "configurable 1-byte vs 2-byte opcode" requirement; second, the "independent command" mechanism (the `S_IND_CMD` phase — command-only, no address/data, with a `mode` field that switches the target to SPI/QSPI) is an almost direct match for the Enter/Exit-QPI (`0x35`/`0xF5`) mechanism used by every chip in the "de facto" set, and can also be reused to send other opcode-only commands that don't change mode — for example the Wrap Boundary Toggle `0xC0` or the Reset Enable/Reset pair `0x66`+`0x99` (sent as two consecutive independent commands) — as long as software sets the `mode` field to the currently-active mode so it doesn't accidentally trigger a switch; third, the runtime `read.cmd`/`write.cmd` swap flow (write the new value, set `req`, wait for `ack`) is exactly the mechanism needed to temporarily issue a Read ID `0x9F` or read/write a Mode Register through the standard read/write pipeline, matching the IP's own software integration guide.

That said, at least two specific gaps remain even within the SDR Quad/SPI scope. First, dummy cycles: per the spec, the `wr_dummy`/`rd_dummy` registers are 32 bits wide, but the FSM currently consumes only the lowest 4 bits (`num[3:0]`), and in QSPI mode this value is further divided by 4 (`num[3:0] >> 2`) before use — meaning the maximum representable QSPI dummy-cycle count is very narrow compared to the 6–8 cycle values common across the "de facto" opcode set (Fast Read Quad `0xEB` = 6 dummy cycles on AP Memory, Espressif, Lyontek, and ISSI alike). This needs clarification of units (bits vs. cycles) and likely a wider field before it can be considered fully compatible with the 0–10 dummy-cycle range required by the MR-based chips (APS1604M, both AP Memory Octal families, ISSI WVQ, Winbond HyperBus). Second: although `cmd_2bytes` matches the letter of the requirement, this research **found no genuine SDR (single-data-rate) PSRAM on the market that actually uses an independent 16-bit opcode space** — the closest case is the 16-bit command representation in ESP-IDF's Octal DDR driver (`0x0000`/`0x8080`/`0x4040`/`0xC0C0`), but that is simply one 8-bit byte repeated across two DDR clock edges, not 65536 genuinely distinct command codes — so the `cmd_2bytes` feature currently has limited practical value until the IP also gains DDR clocking capability.

The biggest gap, exactly as anticipated by the original brief, is **bus architecture**: the IP only has a 4-bit-wide `io_qspi_data` and the entire FSM runs SDR (one sample per SCLK cycle) — there is no 8-bit bus, no RWDS/DQS pin, and no DDR clocking domain. The direct consequence: the IP **cannot** talk to either of AP Memory's Octal families (OctaRAM or Xccela), ISSI's WVO OctalRAM family, or any HyperBus/HyperRAM device (Winbond W955D8MBYA, Infineon/Cypress S27KL0642/S27KS0642) — these devices require different physical hardware (8 data lines, a strobe pin, DDR clocking) that no CSR configuration can emulate. More notably, the IP is **also incompatible with ISSI WVQ (QuadRAM)** despite that family using exactly 4 IO wires just like ordinary QSPI — not because of pin count, but because (a) WVQ runs DDR rather than SDR, and (b) WVQ uses a completely different nibble command set with no Enter/Exit-QPI concept for the IP's existing "independent command" mechanism to leverage. Extending `cmd_2bytes` to 16 bits would not fix this, since the root cause is protocol shape (nibble commands + DDR clocking), not opcode width.

Concrete recommendations to broaden compatibility, ordered by effort: (1) clarify, and if needed widen, the dummy-cycle field actually consumed by the FSM (currently 4 bits) to cover at least 0–10 cycles in both SPI/QSPI modes, avoiding a hidden ceiling when mapping onto the frequency-based Latency-Code tables of APS1604M/Octal/WVQ/HyperBus; (2) document and add explicit test cases for using the "independent command" mechanism to send other common opcode-only commands beyond Enter/Exit-QPI — particularly Wrap Boundary Toggle `0xC0` and the Reset Enable+Reset `0x66`+`0x99` sequence — since the current spec only explicitly describes the mode-switch case, and sending `0xC0` without changing mode is only "theoretically" possible (by setting `mode` to the current value) rather than a confirmed, tested flow; (3) if the goal is to extend into Octal/HyperBus, this is genuinely new architecture (adding 8 IO lines, an RWDS/DQS pin, a DDR clock domain inside the FSM) rather than a small CSR tweak, and should be planned as a separate IP variant rather than a "mode" of the current controller; (4) to additionally cover ISSI WVQ without full Octal/DDR support, a secondary FSM path would be needed to support nibble commands and at least DDR at the address/data phase on the same existing 4 IO wires — a mid-complexity item between the two levels above. Since the spec document does not describe in detail which reference PSRAM model was used to verify the IP (it only lists the `EF_PSRAM_CTRL-1` repository with no specific opcode/protocol information), this research **cannot confirm** which chip in the comparison table that model represents — this should be the next verification step before publishing any official compatibility claim for the IP.

## Conclusion

The 2026 QSPI PSRAM landscape is not "many vendors, one standard" as initially assumed, but rather "one pivot vendor (AP Memory), one opcode standard widely copied by others (Espressif rebrands, Lyontek, ISSI-WVS), and at least three mutually incompatible protocol families coexisting — sometimes within the very same manufacturer." The most practical takeaway for a controller design team is: never infer protocol from a part-number family or IO pin count — APS6408L-3OCx and APS6408L-OBMx share the same first digits but have opposite opcodes; ISSI WVQ and WVS share the "IS66/67" prefix, yet one has Enter/Exit-QPI while the other has no such concept at all despite using the exact same 4 IO wires. For the internal IP, already having `cmd_2bytes`, a flexible independent-command mechanism, and runtime command-swapping puts it in a good position to serve the SDR Quad/SPI segment that dominates the real embedded/IoT market (ESP32, RP2350, most STM32 Discovery kits) — but any claim of "broad compatibility with market PSRAM" needs to state its boundaries explicitly: no Octal, no DDR, no HyperBus, and further verification still needed for ISSI WVQ — rather than leaving these as an implicit assumption.
